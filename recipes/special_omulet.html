The recipe of preparing frenche Special omelet

Description

This is considered the gold standard for chefs. Unlike a standard country-style omelet, a classic French omelet has zero brown color on the outside. It uses high heat and lots of butter. You must shake the pan constantly while stirring the eggs vigorously with a fork to prevent large curds from forming. A smooth, pale yellow exterior rolled into a cigar shape. The inside is creamy and slightly runny (called baveuse). It is often filled with simple herbs like chives or tarragon.

The ingredients
  • Large Eggs
  • 1 tbsp Unsalted Butter
  • Salt & Pepper
  • Fresh Herbs
preparation
  • Whisk the Eggs

    Crack the eggs into a bowl. Season with salt and pepper. Whisk them vigorously with a fork until they are completely yellow and you cannot see any separate whites

  • Heat the Pan

    Place your non-stick pan over medium-high heat. Add the tablespoon of butter. Let it melt and foam.

  • The "Scramble" (Speed is key!)

    Pour the eggs into the pan. Immediately start shaking the pan back and forth with one hand, while stirring the eggs rapidly in circles with the fork in your other hand

  • Stop and Smooth

    As soon as the eggs look like soft custard (mostly set but still wet and slightly runny on top), stop stirring

  • The Roll

    Tilt the pan handle up so the pan is angled downwards away from you.
    Use your fork to carefully roll the top edge of the omelet down toward the center.
    Continue rolling until it looks like a cigar or a cylinder sitting at the bottom edge of the pan.

  • Plate It

    Hold the pan over your plate. Tilt the pan until the omelet rolls out onto the plate, so the seam is on the bottom.
